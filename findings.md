# Analysis: Results and Findings

> **Source:** Sections 5 & 6 of the research paper  
> **Data period:** January 2023 – December 2024  
> **Data type:** Anonymized retail media campaign performance data from a leading digital marketing agency operating in India's quick commerce sector

---

## 5.1 Category-Level Performance: Grocery vs. Gadgets

The most significant finding of this study is a **consistent, statistically meaningful performance gap** between grocery and gadget advertising across all three platform types.

### What the data shows

Grocery ads outperform gadget ads across every platform and every metric. The CTR gap ranges from **1.8× (Google Shopping)** to **4.1× (Meta Stories/Reels)**. This is not simply a creative quality issue — it reflects a fundamental difference in how consumers psychologically approach these two categories.

| Platform & Format | Grocery CTR (%) | Gadget CTR (%) | Grocery Conv. Rate (%) | Gadget Conv. Rate (%) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Marketplace Sponsored Listings | 4.2–6.8 | 1.4–2.7 | 8.5–12.3 | 2.1–4.6 |
| Marketplace Search Ads | 3.9–5.4 | 1.2–2.3 | 7.8–10.1 | 1.8–3.9 |
| Meta Feed Ads (Static) | 2.1–3.4 | 0.7–1.4 | 4.3–6.2 | 0.9–2.0 |
| Meta Dynamic Product Ads | 3.2–4.8 | 1.6–2.9 | 5.8–8.4 | 2.2–4.1 |
| Meta Stories/Reels Ads | 1.8–2.9 | 0.6–1.2 | 3.1–5.0 | 0.7–1.6 |
| Google Search Ads | 4.7–7.2 | 2.3–4.1 | 9.2–14.6 | 3.4–6.8 |
| Google Shopping Ads | 3.8–5.6 | 2.8–4.7 | 7.4–11.2 | 3.9–7.2 |
| Google Display / GDN Retargeting | 1.2–2.1 | 0.5–1.0 | 2.8–4.5 | 1.2–2.7 |

### Key patterns explained

**Why Google Shopping has the smallest gap:**  
Google Shopping bundles multiple trust signals into a single ad unit — product image, price, seller name, and star ratings are all visible before a click. This "pre-built credibility" partially compensates for the trust deficit consumers feel about buying gadgets on quick commerce platforms.

**Why Meta Dynamic Product Ads (DPA) outperform static Meta ads for gadgets:**  
DPA improved gadget CTR by **+110%** and conversion by **+92%** compared to static Meta formats. This confirms that behavioral personalization significantly helps close the trust gap — when an ad shows a product a consumer has already browsed, it feels relevant rather than intrusive.

**Why Google Search Ads show the smallest grocery-gadget gap of all formats:**  
A consumer actively searching for a gadget on Google is already in evaluation or purchase mode. Trust is the *final* barrier for these consumers, not the first. This makes search intent a powerful trust proxy.

## 5.2 Trust Signal Effectiveness (A/B Test Results)

This is the most actionable finding of the study. A/B tests run across marketplace and Meta campaigns tested individual trust signal elements added to baseline gadget ad creatives.

| Trust Signal Element | CTR Uplift | Conv. Rate Uplift | ROAS Improvement | Platform |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Verified Seller Badge | +34% | +41% | +2.1x | Marketplace |
| Transparent Pricing (No Hidden Charges) | +28% | +36% | +1.8x | All |
| Platform Purchase Guarantee Badge | +22% | +44% | +2.4x | Marketplace |
| Clear Return Policy in Ad Copy | +19% | +38% | +2.0x | Meta, Google |
| Star Rating Display (4.0+ stars) | +31% | +29% | +1.6x | Google Shopping, Meta |
| Brand Authorization Certificate Mention | +26% | +33% | +1.9x | Marketplace, Google |
| Warranty Period Prominently Displayed | +17% | +42% | +2.2x | All |
| **All Trust Signals Combined** | **+58%** | **+73%** | **+3.4x** | **All** |

### Critical insight: The synergy effect

The combined trust signal uplift (+58% CTR, +73% conversion) is **far greater than the sum of individual signals**. This means trust in gadget advertising is **holistic** — consumers respond to the overall credibility picture, not just a single badge or label.

### Why the Platform Guarantee Badge drives the highest single-signal conversion uplift (+44%)

This directly addresses the #1 consumer fear for gadget purchases on quick commerce: *"If something goes wrong, will the platform take responsibility?"* A formal guarantee badge answers that question before the consumer even needs to ask it.

## 5.3 Multi-Touch Retargeting: The Trust Journey

The retargeting analysis revealed a dramatic difference in how consumers purchase groceries versus gadgets.

| Metric | Grocery Buyers | Gadget Buyers |
|---|---|---|
| Average ad touchpoints before purchase | 1.8 | 6.4 |
| Primary purchase trigger | Single high-intent ad | Multi-platform retargeting sequence |

### The typical gadget buyer journey (5 steps)

1. **Discovery** — Google Search or Shopping Ad (first awareness)
2. **Evaluation** — Marketplace search (active comparison)
3. **Re-engagement** — Meta Dynamic Product Ad (personalized reminder)
4. **Price check** — Google Shopping (verification)
5. **Conversion** — Marketplace push notification + Meta retargeting ad (final trigger)

This is not a transaction funnel — it is a **trust-building journey**. Each touchpoint reduces perceived risk incrementally until the consumer feels confident enough to purchase.

## 5.4 Advertising Transparency Observations

Platform-level disclosure practices varied significantly:

- **Google Search & Shopping:** Strictest — "Sponsored" labels always visible, merchant identity always clear
- **Marketplace-native:** Most variable — some platforms clearly mark sponsored listings, others use design patterns that consumers mistake for organic results
- **Meta Ads:** Disclosure present but consumer awareness of paid content is lower

Research (Boerman et al., 2017) confirms that transparency disclosures **increase** rather than decrease advertising effectiveness in high-involvement categories. Consumers trust platforms more when they know the ad is paid — not less.

## 6. Discussion

### The trust gap — and why it matters

The core finding is a **trust asymmetry**: grocery advertising benefits from years of habitual purchasing behavior, brand familiarity, and low financial risk. Gadget advertising starts from a deficit because:
- Higher average order value → higher perceived financial risk
- More complex evaluation criteria → more cognitive effort required
- Fear of counterfeit products or poor post-sale service
- Quick commerce's identity as a "grocery app" creates category mismatch expectations

### Three theoretical contributions

1. **Extends the Elaboration Likelihood Model (ELM)** to quick commerce advertising — confirming grocery ads work via peripheral (heuristic) processing while gadget ads require central (analytical) processing
2. **Extends Trust-Commitment Theory** to multi-platform advertising environments
3. **Introduces "trust asymmetry"** as a concept in quick commerce category expansion

### What bridges the trust gap

Based on the data, three things consistently close the gap between grocery-level and gadget-level trust:
- **Credibility architecture** (combined trust signals)
- **Behavioral personalization** (DPA > static ads)
- **Multi-touch retargeting sequences** (6+ touchpoints across platforms)

## Hypotheses — Supported or Not?

| Hypothesis | Verdict | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| H1: Ad transparency → stronger trust effect for gadgets than groceries | Supported | Transparency disclosure data; Google > Marketplace in sponsorship clarity |
| H2: Moderate personalization → trust; excessive personalization → distrust | Supported | Meta DPA +110% CTR vs static; but over-personalization flagged in literature |
| H3: Multi-touch frequency → positive cumulative trust effect for gadgets | Supported | 6.4 average touchpoints vs 1.8 for groceries |
| H4: Perceived credibility is strongest predictor of gadget trust | Supported | Combined trust signals: +73% conversion — largest single effect |
| H5: Platform type moderates advertising-trust relationship | Supported | Marketplace = purchase intent; Meta = social proof; Google = source credibility |
| H6: Trust mediates ad attributes → purchase intention (full for gadgets, partial for groceries) | Supported | Category-level trust asymmetry documented across all KPIs |
